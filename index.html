Platform Lesson

Outsource integration. Not orchestration.

Large procurement becomes platform-like when the same Owner repeatedly connects demand with outside capability under shared rules. BWI can use industry partners for major parts of integration, architecture work and operations. But the Owner still has to know which standards are mandatory, which interfaces must remain open, where dependencies arise and which implementation choices are quietly becoming ecosystem rules.

Integration partners do not automatically “set the standards”. But repeated integration choices can become de-facto standards if the Owner does not govern them consciously.

Explore the PBMC

The model is bounded to BWI's role in connecting Bundeswehr demand with internal and external digital capability.

Guiding question

What becomes scarce when the ecosystem grows?

Technology may become easier to source. The scarce capability can shift to orchestration: making more providers, interfaces and security dependencies work together without losing control.

Consumer · Bundeswehr

Needs secure, ready-to-use digital capability. It brings requirements, priorities and operational context into the ecosystem.

Provider · Vendors

Supply software, hardware, specialist skills and delivery capacity. The roadmap makes potential future demand more visible, while formal access remains procurement-based.

Partner · Integrator

Makes multiple technologies fit the same architecture and operating environment. This role matters more as the number of suppliers and interfaces grows.

Owner · BWI

Combines own capability with market capability, defines governance and architecture boundaries, and bundles contributions into usable services for the Bundeswehr.

Core Value Unit · IT Service

The exchanged value is not a tender or a supplier contract by itself. It is the resulting digital service: software, infrastructure, cloud, cybersecurity, workplace technology or specialist support that becomes usable for the Bundeswehr.
